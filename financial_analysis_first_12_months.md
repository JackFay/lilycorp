### In-Depth Analysis: Structure C (Pre-Close KT & Dead Inventory Adjustment)

Structure C is designed to solve the two biggest financial and operational risks:

#### 1. Pre-Closing Dead Inventory Count (> 2 Years Old = $0)
* **The Problem:** Lily Corp has $750,000 in reported inventory on its balance sheet. In 2025, they wrote off $200k, admitting that $92k was dead stock dating back to 1982. Bob stated during the site visit: *"Some big ticket items like caps for the CD-250 sit on shelves for years."* Borrowing at 8% SBA rates to buy obsolete plastic is a critical financial error.
* **The Mechanism:** The Asset Purchase Agreement stipulates a **joint physical inventory count 5–10 business days before closing**. Any inventory line item older than 24 months (or exceeding 24 months of trailing forward demand) is written down to **$0** and **deducted dollar-for-dollar from the $1,250,000 purchase price**.
* **Financial Impact:** If $100,000 of dead inventory is eliminated, the purchase price drops to $1,150,000, reducing annual SBA debt service by approximately **$13,099/year**.

#### 2. Pre-Close Knowledge Transfer (KT) Window (30–60 Days Pre-Close)
* **The Problem:** Once a retiring seller receives their closing wire, motivation to train collapses.
* **The Mechanism:** Transition training is made a **condition precedent to closing**. During the 30–60 day SBA underwriting window, Jack spends 3–4 days/week on-site in Aurora:
  * Conducting joint warm introductory calls and site visits to Lily Corp's **Top 30 customer accounts**.
  * Documenting Bob's unwritten quoting formulas, ratio specs, and custom dispenser BOMs.
  * Verifying that Dave Jepsen, Dave Kling, Julian, and Maggie are fully confident running daily operations alongside Jack before closing funds are released.

#### 3. Bob Off the Books on Day 1 ($0 Post-Close Payroll)
* Bob has stated he is off payroll Day 1. This is the base case, not a special structure.

## 3. The Trout Facility Lease Proposal & Terms Analysis

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Trout Offer:**  
> *"propose rent for 5 years would at $68,000 annually with the lessee paying taxes, insurance and maintenance"*

### Commercial Evaluation of the Proposal
1. **Base Rent:** $68,000 / year = **$5,666.67 / month**.
   * ~$5.67 to $6.80/sf NNN on an estimated 10,000–12,000 sq ft Aurora facility.
   * **Market Assessment:** In line with Kane County industrial flex rates (~$6–$7/sf NNN). **Because the landlord is the selling family, expect the SBA lender to ask whether $68k NNN is market. Get a broker comp — do not just declare it.** The Trouts reported $0 rent on 2024 and 2025 tax returns because the company owned the building. The $68k is entirely new expense that didn't exist historically.
2. **Triple Net (NNN) Burdens:** Already paid historically by Lily Corp (property taxes $12,097, building maint $21,751, insurance $39,792). The NNN lease formalizes them while adding the $68k base rent.

### 3 Mandatory Lease Terms to Counter-Propose
1. **5+5 Renewal Option:** SBA SOP 50 10 **8.1** (effective Oct 1, 2026) — lease term including options should cover the loan term. Structure as **5-year initial + one 5-year tenant option**, rent escalations capped at 2.5%–3.0% or CPI.
2. **Right of First Refusal (ROFR) to Purchase:** Bob stated: *"They are willing to sell the building."* Include a ROFR granting Jack the right to match any third-party purchase offer.
3. **Facility Condition & Environmental Indemnity:** Landlord certifies roof/HVAC/electrical/foundation in working order. Seller provides environmental indemnity for pre-closing contamination (Phase I ESA).

## 8. Escrow Holdback ($125,000) — Purpose & Limitations

The $125,000 escrow holdback serves specific contractual purposes:

1. **Representation & Warranty Protection:** Covers seller breaches discovered post-close.
2. **Inventory True-Up:** Dollar-for-dollar clawback for obsolete stock found at closing count.
3. **Seller Compliance:** Leverage if Bob disengages on customer intros or lease/ROFR documentation.

> [!CAUTION]
> **The escrow is NOT a rainy-day operating fund.** It is contractually earmarked for purchase-price protection and seller compliance. You cannot run payroll from it when revenue dips. Do not count it as both price protection **and** operating reserve.
