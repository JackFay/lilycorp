*(Grounded in Post-Site Visit Operational Realities & Zero Customer Friction)*

**Objective:** Scale annual revenue from **$1.9M to $2.6M–$2.9M (+35% to +50%)** and unlock **+$150,000 to $300,000 in incremental EBITDA** without alienating existing contractor relationships, changing customer buying habits, or overburdening shop personnel.

## Strategy 1: The "Winter Factory Overhaul & Recertification" Program

### 1. Operational Basis
* Highway, bridge, and civil infrastructure repairs shut down across the Midwest, Northeast, and Rockies from November to March due to sub-freezing temperatures.
* Contractors park their proportioners and dispensers in maintenance yards for 4 months.
* High-pressure injection pumps gum up with cured resin, check valves wear out, and pressure gauges lose calibration over time.
* Shop personnel (Dave Cling, Julian, Greg) experience lower build volumes during winter months.

### 2. The Playbook
* In October, dispatch a formal service campaign to Lily Corp's 10-year historical customer database:
  > *"Avoid costly spring downtime on your bridge contracts. Ship your proportioner to Aurora this winter for a complete Factory Teardown, Ultrasonic Cleaning, Seal Replacement, and 2027 Pressure Calibration."*
* Technicians tear down the fluid sections, replace O-rings, packings, and check valves, test pressure/ratio output, and slap an official **"Factory Certified Inspection"** badge on the unit.
* Offer white-glove freight pickup and return logistics.

### 3. Financial Sizing
* **Price:** Flat-fee tiers:
  * Tier 1 (Basic Seal & Calibration): $1,450
  * Tier 2 (Full Fluid Section Rebuild & Pressure Hoses): $2,850
  * Tier 3 (Complete Overhaul with Motor/Electrical): $3,950
* **Volume:** 40 to 50 machines serviced per winter.
* **Annual Revenue Impact:** **+$75,000 to $150,000** at 70% gross margins.

## Strategy 2: Cross-Compatibility (Selling Molded Parts to Non-Lily Users)

### 1. Operational Basis
* Lily Corp owns 10 injection molds producing ~25 custom plastic and nylon parts (surface ports, corner ports, tubing adapters, static mixers).
* Currently, Bob only sells these items to contractors who already own Lily machines or call the Aurora office directly.
* Surface injection ports and static mixers are **universal commodities**. Contractors using Graco, ChemGrout, or handheld 300ml x 300ml dual-cartridge caulk guns all use the exact same plastic ports and static mixers to inject epoxy into concrete cracks.

### 2. The Playbook
* Package Lily’s proprietary molded nylon surface ports into standardized contractor bulk master cartons:
  * 250-Count Contractor Box (Retail: $185; COGS: ~$28)
  * 1,000-Count Master Jobsite Crate (Retail: $650; COGS: ~$95)
* Clearly label packaging: **"Heavy-Duty Universal Surface Ports — Compatible with Lily, Graco, ChemGrout, and Manual Cartridge Systems."**
* Offer volume tier discounts directly to concrete restoration contractors and regional construction supply branches (White Cap, Brock White, SpecChem distributors).

### 3. Financial Sizing
* Stamping out plastic parts on existing molds costs pennies in raw nylon/polyethylene resin.
* Selling just 200 contractor master cartons per year adds **+$100,000 to $200,000** in high-margin (70%–80%) consumable sales.

## Strategy 3: Chemical Resin Bundling (Authorized Stocking Distributor)

### 1. Operational Basis
* A dispenser or proportioning pump is useless without resin. Every time a contractor buys equipment or ports, they must also source 5-gallon pails or 55-gallon drums of structural epoxy or polyurethane from chemical manufacturers (Sika, Euclid Chemical, MAPEI, Simpson Strong-Tie, Prime Resins).
* Contractors hate issuing multiple POs and paying separate freight carriers for pumps and resins.
* Frequently, contractors ask: *"Can you ship 4 pails of low-viscosity injection epoxy and 2 pails of paste on the same pallet as the pump?"*

### 2. The Playbook
* Apply to become an authorized stocking distributor for 2–3 of the most widely specified concrete crack repair resins (e.g., Sika Sikadur 35 Hi-Mod LV, Euclid Duralprep, or Prime Resins Prime Flex).
* Keep 2 to 4 pallets of fast-moving resin in stock in the Aurora warehouse.
* Offer **"Turn-Key Crack Repair Pallets"**: Dispenser/Pump + 500 Ports + 10 Boxes of Mixers + Required Resin Pails, pre-palletized and shipped on a single bill of lading.

### 3. Financial Sizing
* While chemical distribution carries lower gross margins (25%–35%) than manufactured equipment, it drives **massive ticket sizes ($2,000–$5,000 per order)** with recurring consumable velocity.
* Capturing resin orders on just 20% of existing equipment shipments adds **+$150,000 to $300,000** in top-line volume.

## Strategy 4: Monetizing Idle Injection Press Hours (Contract Tolling)

### 1. Operational Basis
* Site visit verification confirmed: Lily Corp runs batch manufacturing runs for parts (like caps for the CD-250), which then sit in inventory for years.
* Consequently, the injection molding press and material dryer (which received $16,000 in recent upgrades) sit **completely idle for weeks or months at a time**.
* Greg (press technician) is a salaried employee whose machine setup knowledge is fully underutilized during non-production weeks.

### 2. The Playbook
* Offer small-to-medium batch **custom contract molding / tolling services** to regional equipment and industrial component manufacturers in the Aurora/Elgin/Chicagoland industrial corridor.
* Major custom molders require minimum runs of 100,000+ units with $50k tooling costs. Lily Corp can profitably take niche runs of 2,500 to 20,000 parts in nylon, HDPE, or polypropylene where customers already have existing tooling.
* Greg sets up the mold and runs the press during standard downtime.

### 3. Financial Sizing
* Billing press machine time at standard industrial rates ($65–$95/hour + material markup).
* Running the press just 10–15 hours per week of outside contract tolling generates **+$50,000 to $100,000** of near-pure EBITDA, as facility and machine overhead are already sunk.

## Strategy 5: Reviving the High-Margin Equipment Rental Fleet

### 1. Operational Basis
* In 2015, Lily Corp achieved **$215,922 in Rental Revenue** at 85%+ net margins.
* In recent years, this revenue dropped below $40,000 simply because Bob Trout stopped actively marketing or quoting rentals.
* Site visit verification confirmed: Lily Corp has *"quite a few pre-built rental machines"* sitting on shelves right now in Aurora, fully assembled and 100% paid for.

### 2. The Playbook
* Create an active **Rental Fleet Board** for Dave Jepsen and Maggie showing real-time machine availability, model numbers, and weekly/monthly rates.
* **The Rent-to-Own (RTO) Incentive:** Civil contractors bidding on short 2-to-6-week DOT projects frequently resist spending $15k–$20k on a new machine. Offer:
  > *"Rent the unit for $1,250/week. If you keep it, 50% of all rental payments apply directly toward the purchase price."*
* Every rental machine shipped goes out loaded with Lily Corp ports, static mixers, and hoses, guaranteeing recurring consumable orders.

### 3. Financial Sizing
* Renting out an average of 4 to 6 units per month across the country generates **+$100,000 to $180,000/year** in rental cash flow.
* Because the fleet is already built and amortized, gross contribution margin exceeds **85%**.

## Strategy 6: Outbound DOT Bid Letting Scrapers for Dave Jepsen

### 1. Operational Basis
* Bob Trout never conducted outbound sales; orders arrived purely via inbound phone calls and word-of-mouth.
* Dave Jepsen was identified during the site visit as a strong candidate for commercial sales, customer service, and technical training, but lacks structured lead generation.
* State Departments of Transportation (IDOT, INDOT, WisDOT, ODOT, MDOT) publish **every single awarded civil highway, bridge, and infrastructure contract publicly** (bi-weekly state letting results).

### 2. The Playbook
* Implement an automated scraper / alert feed monitoring state DOT award bulletins for specific work codes:
  * *"Concrete Crack Injection"*
  * *"Epoxy Pressure Injection"*
  * *"Substructure Bridge Deck Rehabilitation"*
  * *"Polyurethane Grouting"*
* Every Friday afternoon, Dave Jepsen receives an actionable list of winning contractors:
  * *Contractor Name, Awarded Project Number, Letting Amount, Project Location, Lead Estimator / Project Manager Contact.*
* Dave makes a warm, highly relevant call on Monday morning:
  > *"Hi Mark, congrats on winning the IDOT I-80 bridge letting in Joliet. We noticed there's 3,500 linear feet of structural crack injection specified. We have the approved positive-displacement proportioners and surface ports in stock in Aurora and can deliver directly to your jobsite trailer."*

### 3. Financial Sizing
* Reaching contractors at the exact moment they have won funded state money transforms Dave Jepsen from a reactive clerk into an active sales driver.
* Winning just 1 to 2 additional contractor projects per month adds **+$150,000 to $250,000** in high-ticket machine sales and consumables.
