# Lily Corp: Priority Site Visit Questions
*(Ranked in Order of Importance: Deal Killers, Operational Realities, and Diligence)*

**Target:** Lily Corporation (Aurora, IL)  
**Host:** Robert "Bob" Trout (President)  
**Buyer:** Jack Fay  

---

## Part 1: The Core Deal Killers & Valuation Questions

### 1. The 2025 Inventory Write-Down & Current Age (> 2 Yrs = $0)
> **Question to ask Bob:**  
> *"In your 2025 notes, you wrote down $200k in inventory after a hand count, noting $92k was obsolete stock accumulated since 1982. How do you track inventory age today, and realistically, how much of what is sitting on these shelves right now is older than 2 years?"*
* **What to listen for:** Does he admit they have dead stock, or claim "plastic doesn't go bad"? Watch if he points to an actual tracking method or admits it's just visual guesswork.

---

### 2. Ownership of the 10 Injection Molds (The Core Asset Moat)
> **Question to ask Bob:**  
> *"Are all 10 injection molds owned 100% free-and-clear by Lily Corp, or do any customers have proprietary rights, tooling ownership, or the right to take their molds elsewhere?"*
* **What to listen for:** Any hesitation. If a major customer owns the tooling for parts they buy, that customer can pull their molds on Day 1 and destroy that product line.

---

### 3. The 2024 Revenue Dip (-$600k) vs. The 2026 Rebound
> **Question to ask Bob:**  
> *"Looking at your 10-year financials, Lily Corp was rock-solid around $2.0M–$2.2M in 2022 and 2023, but dipped to $1.6M in 2024 with a net operating loss. What specifically caused that $600,000 decline—did you lose a key customer, were commercial/DOT projects delayed, or did you step back? And what drove the rebound we're seeing back over $2M in 2026?"*
* **What to listen for:** Macro delays (projects deferred due to interest rates) = **Green Flag**. Lost a major customer or competitor undercut prices = **Major Red Flag**. Blaming "bookkeeping errors" for a top-line sales drop = **Evasive**.

---

### 4. Bob’s Commitment to a 6 to 12 Month Transition
> **Question to ask Bob:**  
> *"You hold 40 years of customer trust, pricing formulas, and technical seminar knowledge. I’d need you committed for 6 to 12 months post-sale to do warm account introductions and train me. How does that fit your retirement timeline?"*
* **What to listen for:** Willingness vs. eagerness to bolt. If he resists staying for at least 6 months, customer churn risk goes through the roof.

---

### 5. Internal Successor for Bob’s Role (Can Dave J Step Up as GM?)
> **Question to ask Bob:**  
> *"My goal as an owner is to modernize systems, technology, and operations from an executive level, rather than managing daily phone orders long-term. Looking at your current team—specifically Dave J or Maggie—is there anyone who has the respect, skill, and desire to step up into a General Manager role to run day-to-day operations? Or would I eventually need to recruit an outside operator?"*
* **What to listen for:** Does Bob praise Dave J’s technical and customer command, or claim *"nobody can do what I do"*? If he confirms Dave J (paired with Maggie) can run operations with proper incentives, you have a clear path to being a non-operator tech owner.

---

### 6. Shareholder & Family Alignment (Who Actually Signs the Deal?)
> **Question to ask Bob:**  
> *"I noticed payments to Lily Trout ($86k) and Mike Trout in your P&L, and I’ve been coordinating with John. Who are all the legal shareholders/owners of Lily Corp, and is everyone in the family 100% aligned on selling the business at the $1.25M valuation?"*
* **What to listen for:** **Major deal killer.** If John, Bob's wife Lily, or kids own shares or rely on distributions and aren't fully on board, the deal could fall apart months into legal drafting.

---

### 7. Existing Debt, UCC Liens & COVID SBA EIDL Loans
> **Question to ask Bob:**  
> *"Does Lily Corp currently have any outstanding business bank debt, equipment financing notes, lines of credit, or a COVID-era SBA EIDL loan that has a blanket UCC lien on company assets?"*
* **What to listen for:** **SBA Deal Killer.** If Lily Corp took an SBA EIDL disaster loan (very common in 2020/2021), Chase CANNOT fund a new SBA 7(a) acquisition loan unless the EIDL loan is paid off in full at closing with a clean UCC termination.

## Part 2: Operations, Customers & Equipment

### 8. Customer Concentration & Churn
> **Question to ask Bob:**  
> *"Who are your top 3 to 5 customers by revenue, what percentage of total sales do they represent, and are any of them pushing back on prices or threatening to leave?"*
* **What to listen for:** If any single contractor accounts for >15% of revenue, or if their biggest accounts are personal friends of Bob who might not buy from a new owner.

---

### 9. Product Liability, Safety & Historical Lawsuits
> **Question to ask Bob:**  
> *"Given that your dispensers and proportioning pumps operate under high hydraulic pressure injecting chemicals into civil bridges and buildings, have you ever faced a product liability lawsuit, equipment failure claim, or OSHA citation?"*
* **What to listen for:** High-pressure injection equipment carries inherent risk. You need to know their claims history and what they pay annually for product liability insurance.

---

### 10. Sole-Source Vendor Dependencies
> **Question to ask Bob:**  
> *"Are there any critical raw materials (specific nylon/polyethylene resins, pump motors, specialty check valves) where you rely on a single supplier who could halt production if they had supply issues?"*
* **What to listen for:** Supply chain fragility. If the resin for their 25 molded parts comes from one niche vendor with 6-month lead times, that is a critical operational risk.

---

### 11. Operational Status of Molds & Machinery ($16k Repairs)
> **Question to ask Bob:**  
> *"You noted $16,000 in major mold repairs in 2024/2025 and a new material dryer. Are all 10 molds currently in working condition, who does your tool & die maintenance, and how reliable is the injection molding press?"*
* **What to listen for:** Are any molds currently cracked or producing parts out of tolerance? Who is the outside tool & die shop they rely on?

---

### 12. Documented Setup Sheets vs. Tribal Memory (SOPs)
> **Question to ask Bob:**  
> *"For the 25 molded parts and pump assemblies, are machine recipes (temperatures, cycle times, clamping pressures) and BOMs documented on paper/electronically, or do they live in Dave K and Greg’s heads?"*
* **What to listen for:** Confirms whether you have to spend your first 90 days extracting tribal knowledge before an aging shop employee retires.

---

## Part 3: Legal, Lease & Closing Mechanics

### 13. Financial Reconciliation (QuickBooks vs. Tax Returns)
> **Question to ask Bob:**  
> *"Can you walk me through how the 2024 duplicate inventory entry error happened? When my CPA and lender review the raw QuickBooks file against your 1120-S tax returns, what is the clean, normalized profit we should expect?"*
* **What to listen for:** Defensive attitude vs. transparent acknowledgment of sloppy bookkeeping.

---

### 14. Aurora Facility Lease & Landlord Assignment
> **Question to ask Bob:**  
> *"Who owns this building, what are your current lease terms, and will the landlord formally agree to assign the lease for a 5-to-10-year term matching an SBA loan?"*
* **What to listen for:** If Bob owns the building, ensure rent is fair market value. If a third party owns it, confirm the relationship is healthy and assignable.

---

### 15. Environmental History (SBA Phase I ESA Prerequisite)
> **Question to ask Bob:**  
> *"Because operations involve injection molding, welding, and solvents, Chase will mandate a Phase I Environmental Site Assessment. Has the property ever had chemical spills, underground storage tanks, or EPA/OSHA inspections?"*
* **What to listen for:** Any mention of solvent dumping, floor drain issues, or unpermitted waste disposal that could fail an environmental audit.

---

### 16. Willingness to Sign a Strict 5-Year Non-Compete
> **Question to ask Bob:**  
> *"Are you and John fully prepared to sign a standard 5-year non-compete covering this equipment and seminar space across the country?"*
* **What to listen for:** Immediate agreement. Any hedge is a red flag.

---

### 17. Pre-Closing Joint Physical Inventory Count
> **Question to ask Bob:**  
> *"Before closing, are you comfortable with a joint physical count where any inventory older than 2 years is written down to $0 and deducted from the purchase price?"*
* **What to listen for:** Tests his confidence in his own inventory valuation.

## Part 4: Field Validation: Testing the "6:30 AM Digital Demand" Hypothesis
*(Avoids founder defensiveness by probing real customer behavior instead of asking directly if the business is deficient)*

### A. The "Personal Cell Phone" Test (Ask Bob)
> *"Bob, how often do contractors call your personal cell phone at 6:00 AM or on Sunday nights because they're stuck on a jobsite and realized they ran out of mixing nozzles or ports?"*
* **What to listen for:** If he says *"All the time! My phone never stops ringing,"* demand for 24/7 ordering is 100% verified—it's just currently trapped in Bob's pocket.

### B. The "Monday Morning Voicemail Avalanche" (Ask Maggie Casually)
> *"Maggie, on Monday mornings when you open the office at 8:00 AM, how many voicemails and unread emails from the weekend are usually waiting for you from contractors wanting parts rushed out today?"*
* **What to listen for:** Maggie will give you the unfiltered truth. If Monday mornings are frantic with weekend parts requests, contractors desperately need self-service ordering.

### C. The "Consumable Drop-Off" Test (Ask Bob or Dave J)
> *"When a contractor buys an injection pump or dispenser from you, do they consistently buy all their ongoing static mixers and ports from Lily Corp for the next 5 years? Or do you see them buy the machine here and then buy wear parts elsewhere?"*
* **What to listen for:** If they admit contractors buy pumps from Lily Corp but buy cheap consumables from local distributors, you have proof that convenience and speed beat brand loyalty.

### D. The "Local Supply House" Competitor (Ask Bob)
> *"Who is your biggest competitor for day-to-day accessories—do guys drive to a local construction supply branch like White Cap or buy generic nozzles online from McMaster-Carr?"*
* **What to listen for:** If contractors run to White Cap at 6:30 AM because Lily Corp’s office isn't open, that validates the exact revenue opportunity for mobile QR-code reordering.
