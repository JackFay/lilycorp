# Lily Corp Acquisition: The Top 10 Deal Requirements
*(Ranked in Order of Priority: Most Important to Least Important)*

**Target:** Lily Corporation (Aurora, IL)  
**Asking Price:** $1,250,000 (Includes Inventory)  
**Buyer:** Jack Fay  
**Structure:** Asset Purchase Agreement (APA) with SBA 7(a) Financing  

---

## The Top 10 Requirements (Ranked by Critical Risk)

### 1. QuickBooks Matches the Tax Returns (Financial Truth & Solvency)
* **Priority Tier:** **Deal Killer #1**  
* **The Rule:** Full delivery of the raw QuickBooks backup file (`.QBB`) from 2018 to present, verified against official IRS Form 4506-C transcripts.
* **Why It’s #1:** If the financials are fraudulent or if the 2024/2025 losses and $200k inventory write-down conceal an unviable, money-losing business, **there is no deal**. Furthermore, Chase will not approve an SBA 7(a) loan without tax returns that reconcile directly with the company's operating ledgers.

---

### 2. All 5 Core Employees Agree to Stay (Operational Continuity)
* **Priority Tier:** **Deal Killer #2**  
* **The Rule:** Confidential transition interviews and signed retention commitments with Dave K, Dave J, Maggie M, Julian P, and Greg E before closing.
* **Why It’s #2:** Lily Corp has **zero written SOPs**. These 5 individuals *are* the operating manual. If Dave K (injection mold & shipping) or Dave J (sales & technical support) quits upon Bob's exit, the business grinds to an immediate halt.

---

### 3. Bob Stays 6 to 12 Months to Train You (Key-Man Transition)
* **Priority Tier:** **Revenue Retention**  
* **The Rule:** Bob signs a binding transition and consulting agreement (full-time for months 1–3, part-time/advisory for months 4–12).
* **Why It’s #3:** Bob holds 40 years of customer relationships, custom equipment quoting formulas, and trade seminar credibility in his head. Without a structured, warm customer handover, customer churn could wipe out 30%–50% of revenue in Year 1.

---

### 4. Proof of Inventory Age (> 2 Years = $0) & Valuation True-Up
* **Priority Tier:** **Valuation & Cash Protection**  
* **The Rule:** SKU-level inventory report with original receipt dates and last date sold. Anything sitting for **more than 24 months is valued at $0**. Final purchase price drops dollar-for-dollar for dead stock or missing inventory.
* **Why It’s #4:** The $1.25M asking price includes inventory. In 2025, they admitted to writing off $92,169 of obsolete stock that accumulated since 1982. You cannot borrow money at 10% SBA interest to pay for decades of hoarded, unsellable plastic.

---

### 5. Lily Corp 100% Owns All 10 Injection Molds (The Core Asset Moat)
* **Priority Tier:** **Asset Integrity**  
* **The Rule:** Clear legal title proving that all **ten (10) injection molds** producing the ~25 custom parts belong free-and-clear to Lily Corp (not owned by customers or third-party tooling shops).
* **Why It’s #5:** These molds are the proprietary manufacturing engine of the company. If customers own the tooling, they can pull their molds and walk away at any time, instantly destroying your product catalog.

---

### 6. Proof the Manufacturing Process Works and Is Documented (No "Ghost" Products)
* **Priority Tier:** **Production Viability**  
* **The Rule:** Full Bills of Materials (BOMs) with active vendors, written machine recipes (temperatures, pressures, cycle times), and a mandatory 1–2 day on-site live demonstration of mold changeovers and equipment testing.
* **Why It’s #6:** Advertised catalog items are worthless if critical parts are discontinued or if the setup knowledge exists only in Greg's head. Any SKU not produced in 24 months must be proven operational or removed from valuation.

---

### 7. A Strict 5-Year Non-Compete From Bob & John Trout
* **Priority Tier:** **Competitive Protection**  
* **The Rule:** Comprehensive 5-year non-compete and non-solicitation agreement covering the continental US across all products, machinery, and educational seminars in Lily Corp’s niche.
* **Why It’s #7:** Prevents the sellers from pocketing $1.25M and immediately launching a competing venture, consulting for a rival, or poaching their historical contractor accounts.

---

### 8. Stable Long-Term Lease on the Aurora Facility & Phase I Environmental
* **Priority Tier:** **Physical Infrastructure**  
* **The Rule:** Formal landlord consent to lease assignment (or a new 5-to-10-year lease matching the SBA loan duration), plus a clean Phase I Environmental Site Assessment (ESA).
* **Why It’s #8:** Relocating heavy machinery, injection molding presses, and welding rigs would cost $100k+ and cause catastrophic operational downtime. The SBA also strictly mandates Phase I environmental clearance for chemical and molding facilities.

---

### 9. Clean Bill of Health on Taxes & Liens (No Successor Liability)
* **Priority Tier:** **Legal Shield**  
* **The Rule:** Certificate of Clean Standing from the Illinois Department of Revenue, IDES unemployment clearance, and clean state/county UCC-1 lien searches.
* **Why It’s #9:** Ensures you don't inherit unpaid state sales taxes, payroll tax liabilities, or secret creditor liens against the machinery and inventory you just bought.

---

### 10. A 10%–15% Cash Holdback (Escrow) for Year 1
* **Priority Tier:** **Post-Closing Safety Net**  
* **The Rule:** $125,000 to $187,500 held in third-party escrow (or a forgivable seller note) for 12 months post-closing.
* **Why It’s #10:** It is your final line of defense. While not a prerequisite to operating the business on Day 1, it guarantees you have immediate cash recourse if hidden liabilities, unbilled vendor invoices, or defective warranty batches emerge during your first year.

---
