Fair point, and I appreciate you asking directly. Let me lay out the exact math and requirements the lenders are working from so there is zero guesswork.

Under SBA rules, lenders cannot underwrite based on 2026 run-rates alone. They are required to underwrite against the filed Form 1120-S tax returns from 2024 and 2025, and the business must hit at least a 1.25x Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR).

Here is the exact formula both lenders used on Lily's numbers:

1. Starting Point (2024 Tax Return): Lily reported an Ordinary Business Loss of -$16,612.
2. Add-Backs: They added back depreciation, interest, and family salaries (totaling ~$260,000).
3. Mandatory Bank Deductions: Per SBA lending guidelines, the bank must deduct two new ongoing expenses that sit ahead of debt service:
   - The proposed commercial facility lease: -$68,000/year (NNN).
   - Buyer living wage / replacement general manager salary: -$100,000/year.

After those deductions, the cash flow available to pay bank debt in 2024 is approximately $85,000. 

To meet the bank's required 1.25x coverage ($85,000 / 1.25), maximum annual debt service cannot exceed roughly $68,000/year. 

On a standard 10-year SBA loan at current interest rates (~9.5%), a $68,000 annual payment caps the maximum senior bank loan at roughly $450,000 to $500,000. 

That is why an $850k to $1M+ bank loan was declined. The bank simply cannot write a loan that large against the historical tax returns.
