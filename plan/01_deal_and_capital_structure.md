The capital structure and deal architecture are governed by four commercial and financial realities:
1. **Historical Tax Cash Flow Floor & Senior Debt Cap:** In 2024, Lily Corporation reported an Ordinary Business Loss of -$16,612. 2. **Quality of Earnings & Inventory Double-Count Reconciliation [DILIGENCE_QOE_03]:** In 2025, Lily recorded an inventory write-down of $200,387.39 (Line 5345). Diligence confirms this write-down comprised $108,218.00 in 2024 duplicate receipt debits and $92,169.39 in cumulative dead stock accumulated over 44 years. If underwriters add back the full $200,387 to 2025 earnings without retroactively correcting 2024 for the $108,218 COGS understatement, historical earnings are double-counted. Restating 2024 drives its CFADS from positive $75,285 down to negative **-$32,933 (-$33k)**. This module establishes the definitive forensic bridge, independent pre-closing count protocol, and purchase agreement Net Working Capital (NWC) target to resolve this risk permanently.
3. **Closing the $296,692 Financing Deficit [DEAL_FIN_GAP_01]:** Total unadjusted acquisition uses initially required $1,300,000 ($1,250,000 headline purchase price plus a $50,000 shop floor working capital injection). Identified initial sources totaled $1,003,308 ($443,308 senior SBA loan, $60,000 buyer cash equity injection, and a $500,000 base seller note placed on 24-month standby), leaving an unfunded deficit of **$296,692.00**. This gap is definitively resolved prior to closing through a two-part, SBA-compliant structure:
   - **Part 1: $108,000 Pre-Closing Purchase Price Adjustment (Reduction of Uses):** Grounded in the verified 2024 QuickBooks duplicate-entry debit error admitted in writing by the seller's CPA, reducing the contractual purchase price from $1,250,000 to **$1,142,000**, and reducing total project uses to **$1,192,000.00**.

Tranche B incorporates an SBA-compliant downward adjustment mechanism (reverse earnout) tied to a $1.05M annual Gross Profit hurdle, fully preserving Bob Trout's valuation upside while eliminating Day-1 cash deficits and clearing SBA SOP 50 10 rules.
4. **The Month 25 Seller Note Debt Cliff ($572k Accrued Principal vs. $85k CFADS) [DEAL_NOTE_CLIFF_02]:** A $500,000 core seller note placed on 24 months of full SBA standby at 7.00% annual compounding interest grows to **$572,450** at Month 25. This document incorporates a baseline 10-year post-standby amortization schedule ($79,760/year) shielded by a non-negotiable **1.35x DSCR Corporate Protection Covenant** with automatic interest-only and payment-in-kind (PIK) conversion triggers.

### D. Detailed Mathematical Architecture of the Performance-Adjusted Standby Seller Note ($188,692 Tranche B)

The performance adjustment is engineered to protect Jack Fay against debt service on underperforming volume while giving Bob Trout full realization of his note principal if the business meets historical baseline gross margins.

#### 1. Operational Rationale for Gross Profit Hurdle
* **Why Revenue Fails:** Rewarding revenue creates perverse incentives. An operator could sell $500,000 of low-margin commodity resins or unbranded equipment at a 15% margin, hitting revenue targets while burning shop cash.
* **Why Net Income Fails:** Net income invites endless disputes regarding overhead allocations, buyer living wages, vehicle expenses, depreciation schedules, and facility lease payments. Bob Trout will distrust net income, fearing the buyer will "expense away" his retirement note.
* **The Gross Margin Alignment:** Gross Profit ($\text{Revenue} - \text{COGS}$) isolates the true operational engine of Lily Corporation: the **65% to 75% gross margins** on in-house molded injection ports (LPP, LPT), surface discs, and mixing nozzles. It aligns both parties: Bob benefits when Lily sells high-margin consumables, and Jack retains 80 cents of every incremental gross profit dollar.

#### 2. Operating Rules & Downward Forgiveness Formula
* **Standby Period:** Months 1–24 (Zero principal, zero interest).
* **Active Measurement Period:** Four (4) consecutive 12-month periods commencing at Month 13 post-closing (Years 2, 3, 4, and 5). *(Year 1 is excluded from performance testing to allow operational stabilization).*
* **Annual Gross Profit Hurdle Rate:** **$1,050,000.00** per fiscal year.
  * *Historical Justification:* Lily Corporation generated Gross Profit of $1,018,400 in 2022 and $956,835 in 2023, before plunging to $704,224 in 2024 and $567,962 in 2025. Setting the hurdle at $1.05M mandates that the business must surpass historical baseline performance before Tranche B principal is fully validated.
* **Downward Forgiveness Formula:**
  If realized Gross Profit in any measurement year $t$ falls below $1,050,000.00, the outstanding principal of Tranche B is reduced by an annual forgiveness deduction:
  $$\text{Principal Forgiveness}_{\text{Year } t} = 0.20 \times \max\left(0, \; \$1,050,000.00 - \text{Gross Profit}_{\text{Year } t}\right)$$
  *(Subject to a maximum cumulative downward forgiveness cap of $188,692.00).*
* **Full Note Retention:** In any measurement year where realized Gross Profit equals or exceeds $1,050,000.00, zero principal is forgiven, and the full pro-rata principal balance is retained.
