A subordinated seller note can provide up to 50% of this requirement (5.0%, or $65,000), provided it is placed on **complete standby (zero principal and zero interest) for the entire 10-year term of the SBA loan** (Seller Note Tranche A).

    * Standby accrual or interest-only conversion under the covenant **does not constitute an event of default**, acceleration, or foreclosure trigger while Senior SBA debt remains fully current.

### 4.1 Midwest Civil Construction Seasonality Mechanics & Working Capital Lag
Lily Corporation's revenue does not flow evenly across the calendar year. The company's core end markets—civil bridge deck repair, highway dowel bar retrofit, DOT structural seismic rehabilitation, and commercial concrete crack injection—are strictly outdoor operations dictated by ambient temperature specifications:

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| MIDWEST CIVIL CONSTRUCTION SEASONALITY PROFILE (ILLINOIS / INDIANA / WISCONSIN / IOWA)             |
+-------------------+--------------------+------------------------+----------------------------------+
| Calendar Period   | Monthly Rev Range  | % of Annual Volume     | Operational Driver               |
+-------------------+--------------------+------------------------+----------------------------------+
| Q1 (Jan - Mar)    | $70k - $105k       | ~14.5% (Trough)        | Sub-40°F DOT freeze; cure halts  |
| Q2 (Apr - Jun)    | $155k - $215k      | ~29.5% (Mobilization)  | Spring thaw; DOT lettings start  |
| Q3 (Jul - Sep)    | $195k - $225k      | ~34.0% (Peak Season)   | Peak bridge repair & paving      |
| Q4 (Oct - Dec)    | $102k - $180k      | ~22.0% (Wind-Down)     | Late joint seal; winter shutdown |
+-------------------+--------------------+------------------------+----------------------------------+
```

1. **The Q1 Freeze / Winter Shutdown (January – March):**
   * Epoxy resins and structural polyurethanes cannot cure at ambient concrete temperatures below 40°F (4°C) without expensive heated enclosures. IDOT, INDOT, and WisDOT bridge construction shuts down across Illinois, Wisconsin, Iowa, and Indiana from late December through late March.
   * Contractor purchasing contracts to near-dormant levels. Historical monthly sales drop to a trough of **$70,000 – $95,000/month** in January and February (averaging ~$85,000–$90,000/mo across Q1), representing roughly **14.5% of annual revenue**.
2. **The Spring Thaw & Project Mobilization (April – May):**
   * State DOT highway lettings activate, contractors pull equipment from winter storage, order seal overhaul kits, replace lost injection ports, and rent CD-250 proportioners for major bridge deck overlay contracts. Monthly sales jump to **$150,000 – $185,000/month**.
3. **The Peak Civil Repair Season (June – October):**
   * Maximum construction activity. Highway bridge overlays, airport runway dowel bar grouting, and parking structure deck rehabilitations run on double shifts.
   * Monthly revenue surges to **$185,000 – $245,000/month** (representing ~55% of annual volume). Consumable injection ports, snappy tees, and static mixers churn at peak margins (65–75%).
4. **The Late Autumn Wind-Down (November – December):**
   * Contractors race to finish highway joints before winter freeze; sales taper from $145,000 in November down to $85,000–$105,000 in December as year-end shutdowns begin.
5. **The 45-Day Working Capital Collection Lag (The Cash Squeeze Engine):**
   * Civil general contractors and national supply houses (White Cap, Sika, Euclid) do not buy on retail credit cards. Transactions occur against corporate Purchase Orders under **Net 30 to Net 60 payment terms**.
   * Average Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) sits between **42 and 52 days** (modeled at a weighted 45-day lag: 50% collected in month following billing, 50% collected two months following billing).
   * *Critical Liquidity Implication:* When sales plummet in December ($95k–$105k) and January ($75k–$85k), cash collections in February and March drop to seasonal lows ($75k–$90k), exactly when fixed debt service, NNN rent, GM salary, and shop heating/gas utilities hit full force.
6. **The Raw Materials Pre-Buy Timing vs. On-Hand Inventory Cushion:**
   * Manufacturing injection-molded consumables and machining proportioner pumps for the April/May surge requires having materials ready in February and March.
   * Crucially, Lily holds over **$500,000 in raw materials, machined castings, fittings, and resin pellets on-site**. During Q1, Dave Kling and Greg draw directly from existing shop inventory, limiting new cash purchases until peak collections arrive in May and June.

### 4.3 Breakeven Cash Flow Analysis & Structural Leverage

$$\text{Monthly Cash Breakeven Revenue} = \frac{\text{Fixed Monthly Cash Commitments}}{\text{Contribution Margin Ratio (Blended GM)}}$$

In Q1 (Jan–Mar), baseline monthly sales average only $85,000 to $105,000, creating an unavoidable winter cash deficit that must be bridged by working capital reserves or peak-season cash retained on the balance sheet.

### 4.7 Month 25 Seller Note Unfreezing & 1.35x DSCR Covenant Stress Mechanics

At Month 25, the 24-month standby agreement mandated by SBA guidelines expires. The $500,000 subordinated note, bearing 7.0% annual interest, compounds to **$572,450.00**.

#### The Mechanics of the 1.35x DSCR Protective Covenant:
The purchase agreement must mandate that seller note repayment is subject to a trailing quarterly Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) test:
$$\text{DSCR} = \frac{\text{Net Operating Income (EBITDA)} - \text{NNN Lease} - \text{GM Living Wage} - \text{Baseline Capex}}{\text{Senior SBA Debt Service} + \text{Proposed Seller Note Debt Service}} \ge 1.35$$

#### Scenario A: Base Case Performance (Month 25)
* Trailing 12-Month EBITDA: ~$385,000
* Minus NNN Lease ($68,000) & GM Salary ($100,000) & Maintenance Capex ($25,000): $192,000 CFADS
* Total Senior SBA Debt Service: $68,836/year ($5,736.37/month)
* Seller Note Amortization (10-Year Schedule @ 7.0% on $572,450): $79,759/year ($6,646.61/month)
* Combined Total Debt Service: $148,595/year
* **Resulting DSCR:**
  $$\text{DSCR} = \frac{\$192,000}{\$148,595} = \mathbf{1.29\times} \quad (\text{Slightly below 1.35x covenant hurdle})$$
* **The Automatic Covenant Adjustment:**
  * Because 1.29x is below the 1.35x hurdle, the Seller Note automatically converts to **Interest-Only ($3,339.29/month)** for Q1 of Year 3.
  * Under Interest-Only ($40,071/yr), combined debt service drops to $108,907/yr.
  * **Adjusted DSCR:**
    $$\text{DSCR} = \frac{\$192,000}{\$108,907} = \mathbf{1.76\times} \quad (\text{Compliant; fully protects working capital})$$
  * As summer revenues surge in Q2/Q3 of Year 3, DSCR rises to >1.85x, automatically reinstating full 10-year principal amortization without requiring lender or seller renegotiation.

#### Scenario B: Downside Performance (-15% Revenue Contraction in Year 3)
* Trailing 12-Month EBITDA: ~$235,000
* Minus NNN Lease ($68k) & GM Salary ($100k) & Barebones Capex ($12k): $55,000 CFADS
* Senior SBA Debt Service: $68,836/year
* **Senior Debt Coverage Alone:**
  $$\text{DSCR}_{\text{Senior}} = \frac{\$55,000}{\$68,836} = \mathbf{0.80\times} \quad (\text{Severe Stress})$$
* **The Covenant Enforcement:**
  * Under the 1.35x DSCR covenant, **Seller Note principal AND interest payments are 100% frozen on Standby**.
  * Zero cash leaves the company toward Bob Trout's note until corporate CFADS recovers above 1.35x senior debt service.
  * *Vital Protection:* Bob Trout cannot foreclose, file UCC claims, or force bankruptcy while Senior SBA debt is kept current.

### 4.8 Working Capital Protection Playbook & Operating Line of Credit (LOC) Strategy

While operational mitigations allow survival on paper, operating a $1.9M manufacturing company with only $8,489 in trough cash cushion leaves zero margin for equipment breakdowns (e.g., an unexpected $12k injection press hydraulic pump replacement or screw barrel seizure).

#### The 3-Pillar Liquidity Protection Protocol:

1. **Establishment of a $100,000 – $150,000 Working Capital Line of Credit (Closing Prerequisite):**
   * *Structure:* Negotiated concurrently with the Senior SBA 7(a) acquisition lender (or a local Aurora community lender such as Old Second National Bank or First Centennial Bank).
   * *Collateral:* Subordinated lien on accounts receivable and inventory (subject to SBA intercreditor agreement).
   * *Utilization Protocol:* The line of credit is strictly designated as a **Seasonal Buffer Facility**, drawn only between January 15 and April 30 to fund raw material procurement ahead of DOT spring bid mobilizations, and fully repaid from peak summer cash flows by August 31.
2. **The "Winter Pre-Build" Counter-Cyclical Shop Schedule:**
   * Instead of slowing shop hours in January/February, Dave Kling and Greg utilize the slow shipping period to conduct annual preventative maintenance on Molds #1–10 and pre-build 500 units of high-runner LPP ports, snappy tees, and static mixer manifolds using raw plastic resin already sitting on shelves.
   * When orders flood in during May and June, shop capacity is 100% focused on rapid fulfillment rather than choked by manufacturing backlogs.
3. **Consumable Gross Margin Expansion (The 70% Profit Core):**
   * Proprietary molded accessories (LPP injection ports, LPT snappy tees, CD-250 resin caps) yield 65% to 75% gross margins.
   * For every $50,000 increase in direct consumable sales captured via contractor reordering, Lily generates **$35,000 in net cash contribution**—the most efficient defense against civil seasonality.
