# What to Ask Bob For (And Why)

## Request #1: The COGS Reconciliation (This Is the Deal)

**Ask for:** A single QuickBooks Profit & Loss Detail report, **by account**, for **calendar years 2023, 2024, 2025, and 2026 YTD** — all on one page or one Excel export. Specifically, you need these COGS sub-accounts broken out individually:

| Account # | Account Name |
|---|---|
| 5000 | Cost of Goods Sold (the parent/catch-all) |
| 5110 | Purchases – Parts |
| 5120 | Purchases – Accessories |
| 5210 | Direct Labor – Manufacturing |
| 5340 | Freight Out |
| 5345 | Inventory Adjustment |
| 5480 | Shipping & Packaging |

Plus total revenue for each period.

**How to phrase it:**
> "Bob, to finalize my financing package I need a P&L export from QuickBooks showing the individual COGS sub-accounts (not rolled up into a single line). Specifically accounts 5000, 5110, 5120, 5210, 5340, 5345, and 5480 — with revenue at the top — for 2023, 2024, 2025, and 2026 year-to-date. Can you run that as a P&L by account and email me the PDF or Excel?"

**Why it matters:**
- Right now, two different QuickBooks exports give two different COGS totals for 2025 ($1.24M vs $1.63M). The reports you've received so far collapse sub-accounts, hiding where the money actually goes.
- The entire deal valuation hinges on whether "variable product cost" (everything except labor) is **40% of revenue or 45%**. That's a **$95,000/year difference** in cash flow at $1.90M.
  - At **40%**: the deal at $1.25M is tight but defensible (+$117k surplus).
  - At **45%**: the deal at $1.25M barely breaks even (+$22k surplus — one bad quarter and you're negative).
- You can't negotiate price, you can't set a walk-away number, and you can't tell Chase what the business earns without this report.
- Account 5000 specifically is the one that was missed in earlier analysis. It had **$121k in 2025** that wasn't showing up in the "Parts & Accessories" subtotal.

**What to look for when you get it:**
1. Add up 5000 + 5110 + 5120 + 5340 + 5480 (everything except 5210 labor and 5345 inventory adjustment) for each year.
2. Divide by that year's revenue.
3. If the answer is **35–40%** across multiple years → the deal has a real safety margin at $1.25M.
4. If the answer is **43–46%** → $1.25M is full price and you need to negotiate down or walk.
5. Ignore 5345 (inventory adjustment) for the rate calculation — it's a one-time write-down/write-up, not a recurring cost.

## Request #2: Real Estate Ownership

**Ask for:** Clarification on who owns the land and building, and whether they are included in the $1.25M asset purchase.

**How to phrase it:**
> "Bob, the 12/31/25 balance sheet shows land, building, and leasehold improvements as company assets. Is the real estate included in the $1.25M purchase price, or is the family retaining the building and leasing it back to the business? I need to know for the SBA application — they'll ask about collateral and related-party leases."

**Why it matters:**
- If the building is a company asset and you're buying all assets for $1.25M, then the Trout family can't also charge you $68k/year rent on it — you'd already own it.
- If the family is retaining the building (excluding it from the APA), that's a **related-party lease** that the SBA lender will scrutinize. They'll want a broker opinion of value confirming $68k NNN is market rate.
- If the building is excluded, the $1.25M price should be **lower** because you're getting fewer assets.
- This also affects SBA collateral. If you own the building, it's collateral for the loan. If you don't, the lender has less security and may want more equity or a personal guarantee on the real estate from the Trouts.

## Request #3: 2026 Rental / Sub-Lease Income Detail

**Ask for:** Detail on where the "rental income" line comes from, and what the current status is.

**How to phrase it:**
> "Bob, the 2025 P&L shows about $163k in rental income and 2026 YTD is only $55k. Is that a tenant subleasing space in the building? Are they still there? What's the lease term?"

**Why it matters:**
- The 2026 YTD revenue figure ($1.474M through Sep 16 → $2.08M annualized) may include **rental income that's evaporating**. If $108k/year in rental income disappeared, the actual product revenue run-rate is lower than it looks.
- If there's a subtenant, you need to know: does that sublease survive the sale? Does the Trout family's $68k NNN lease allow subleasing? Is the subtenant paying Bob directly or through the company?

## Request #4: Employee Census Verification

**Ask for:** Confirmation that the 4-person roster (Dave J, Dave K, Julin, Maggie) is complete and current.

**How to phrase it:**
> "Bob, just confirming — the four employees on the census (Dave Jepsen, Dave Kling, Julin Perez, Maggie Montes) are the full active roster, correct? Greg E came up in earlier conversations as a press operator — is he still involved in any capacity, or have his duties been fully absorbed?"

**Why it matters:**
- If Greg is gone and his work is absorbed, your $400k payroll number is right.
- If you need to hire a press operator post-close, that's **$60–$70k/year** — which at 45% variable and $1.90M revenue wipes out the entire $22k surplus and puts you negative.
