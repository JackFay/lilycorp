THE PLAIN-ENGLISH EXECUTIVE BRIEF

What the Hell Does Lily Corporation Actually Do?

You don't need a chemical engineering degree to understand this company. Lily is a 44-year-old manufacturing cash machine built around a classic "Razor & Blade" business model in heavy civil infrastructure repair.

01
🌉

The Real-World Problem

Concrete bridges, dams, airport runways, and parking garages crack and leak.

When concrete cracks, rain and road salt seep inside, freeze, expand, and rust the internal steel rebar. If ignored, the bridge collapses or the basement floods.

The Solution: Civil contractors drill along the crack, seal the surface, and pump high-pressure chemical resin (epoxy for strength, polyurethane for water cutoff) deep into the structure until it is welded solid.
02
⚡

The Razor & The Blade

Lily sells the pumps, but makes its real fortune on the plastic ports.

THE RAZOR (EQUIPMENT)

CD-250 & CD-15 Pumps

Contractors buy a $15,000–$25,000 precision proportioning pump once every 5–10 years. It mixes resin Part A and Part B at an exact 1:1, 2:1, or 3:1 ratio under 2,000 PSI pressure.

~45–55% Margin
THE BLADE (THE CASH ENGINE)

Molded Plastic Ports & Mixers

For every single pump, contractors buy tens of thousands of plastic injection ports, caps, and static mixers every month. They are glued over the crack, injected, and thrown away.

65%–75% Gross Margin!
03
🏆

Why Contractors Choose Lily Over Graco

Graco builds giant $60k electronic trailers. Lily builds indestructible field tools.

  • Air-Powered Simplicity: Lily machines run on standard air compressors. No 240V high-voltage generators needed on a muddy bridge deck.
  • Confined Space Portability: A 50-lb Lily skid fits through a 24-inch manhole or subway vault where a giant Graco trailer cannot go.
  • Rebuildable in 20 Minutes: If epoxy cures inside a Lily pump, Julian or the contractor can unscrew the fluid block with a simple wrench, clean it in solvent, and resume pumping.
04
💵

How Customers Buy (No Web Stores)

Construction sales do not happen through fancy e-commerce apps.

Contractor superintendents call Dave Jepsen on the phone. Dave calculates the resin viscosity, advises port spacing, and quotes the order.

Orders are placed via Net 30/60 Purchase Orders either directly or through major commercial distributors like White Cap, Sika, and Euclid Chemical.

⚠️ Golden Rule: Never force civil contractors into an online shopping cart. They want Dave Jepsen answering the phone at 07:00 AM.

CONTRACTOR & CHEMICAL INJECTION CHEAT SHEET

Jargon Decoder: Industry Terms in Plain English

When Dave Jepsen, Bob Trout, or a civil contractor talks to you, here is what they actually mean.

Surface Port (LPP)
Consumable Plastic Part

A flat-bottomed plastic injection fitting with a one-way ball or nipple. Contractors epoxy it directly over a crack on a concrete wall or bridge deck so they can inject resin.

Why Jack cares: Molded on Greg's press for ~$0.08 each; sells for ~$0.85–$1.20 in volume (75%+ GM).
Mechanical Packer
High-Pressure Fitting

A steel/brass cylinder with a rubber compression sleeve. Contractors drill a hole into solid concrete at a 45-degree angle to intersect a deep crack, insert the packer, and tighten a hex nut to expand the rubber seal for pressures up to 3,000+ PSI.

Why Jack cares: Lily sells these to heavy civil / geotechnical crews stopping massive water gushes in dams and subway tunnels.
1:1 vs. 2:1 Mixing Ratio
Chemical Proportioning

Chemical resins consist of Part A (resin) and Part B (hardener/catalyst). If the formula requires equal parts, it is 1:1. If it requires two parts resin to one part hardener, it is 2:1.

Why Jack cares: Lily machines (CD-250) control this ratio mechanically using synchronized dual pump cylinders machined by Dave Kling.
Static Mixer Tube
Consumable Accessory

A clear plastic tube containing a spiral baffle core. Part A and Part B enter unmixed from separate hoses, and the spiral baffles fold the chemicals 24 to 32 times so they exit fully blended.

Why Jack cares: Once resin cures inside, the mixer is thrown away. High-volume consumable reorders every week.
Viscosity & Centipoise (cP)
Chemical Property

The thickness of the liquid. Water is 1 cP. Motor oil is ~500 cP. Peanut butter is ~250,000 cP.

Why Jack cares: Hairline bridge cracks require ultra-low viscosity epoxy (100–300 cP) to penetrate deep. Wide foundation cracks take thick paste (5,000+ cP). Dave Jepsen matches the pump to the cP.
Pot Life & Gel Time
Chemical Property

The number of minutes before mixed resin cures rock-solid. Some fast polyurethanes react and foam in 45 seconds when contacting water. Some structural epoxies have a 45-minute pot life.

Why Jack cares: Lily proportioners keep Part A and Part B separated until the very tip of the static mixer, preventing resin from curing inside expensive machine pumps.
Cincinnati Milacron Press
Shop Equipment

The large hydraulic/toggle plastic injection molding machine in Lily's shop operated by Greg. It melts plastic resin pellets and injects them under tons of pressure into steel molds to produce ports and caps.

Why Jack cares: This single machine is the heart of Lily's consumable gross profit. Keeping it maintained and running is priority #1.
Hydrostatic Test Bench (2,200 PSI)
Quality Assurance

A dedicated pressure-testing station where assembled fluid manifolds and pump blocks are pressurized with oil/water up to 2,200 PSI to prove there are zero leaks or weld porosity.

Why Jack cares: Greg tests every pump here before crating. It guarantees zero customer returns or warranty failures in the field.
Formulators (Sika, Euclid, Master Builders)
Chemical Partners

The multi-billion-dollar chemical manufacturers who make the resins and epoxies. They do not want to manufacture pumps; they recommend Lily equipment to contractors because Lily pumps reliably shoot their chemicals.

Why Jack cares: Dave Jepsen maintains these formulator relationships. They are Lily's primary source of inbound equipment and port referrals.
White Cap
Primary Distributor

The largest specialty concrete and construction distributor in North America with 400+ branches. Contractors go there for rebar, concrete tools, and injection ports.

Why Jack cares: White Cap buys Lily ports and accessories in large wholesale master-cartons on Net 30/60 terms.
